# Research Protocol — DigiUMKM

## 1. Proposed study
**Design and Evaluation of a Gamification-Based Digital Payment Adoption Platform for Sustainable QRIS Use among Rural MSMEs in East Java, Indonesia**

## 2. Research problem
UMKM rural may face barriers to adopting and sustaining QRIS use, including limited digital skills, perceived complexity, low perceived value, security concerns, and lack of ongoing support. DigiUMKM is designed as a gamified support platform; its usefulness and effects must be evaluated rather than assumed.

## 3. Research objectives
1. Identify user and technology requirements for QRIS adoption support among rural MSMEs.
2. Design and develop DigiUMKM as a technology artifact.
3. Evaluate usability, usefulness, and engagement.
4. Test relationships among gamification, reward value, engagement, continuance intention, and QRIS use.
5. Compare outcomes with a non-gamified educational intervention if feasible.

## 4. Method
A mixed evaluation using Design Science Research (DSR), usability testing, and a quantitative user study.

### DSR stages
1. Problem identification and motivation.
2. Define objectives for the solution.
3. Design and development.
4. Demonstration with target users.
5. Evaluation.
6. Communication through the journal article.

### Suggested field stages
- Discovery interviews: approximately 10–15 UMKM owners/managers.
- Expert review: approximately 3–5 experts in technology management, UX, digital payment, or MSME development.
- Usability pilot: approximately 15–30 users.
- Main study: sample size determined by a priori power analysis; 150–250 may be a planning range, not a universal rule.
- Follow-up behavior observation: 4–8 weeks if practical.

## 5. Proposed conceptual constructs
- Gamification Features (GF)
- Perceived Reward Value (PRV)
- Perceived Ease of Use (PEOU)
- Perceived Usefulness (PU)
- User Engagement (UE)
- Continuance Intention (CI)
- Sustainable QRIS Use (SQU)

## 6. Proposed hypotheses
- H1: Gamification features positively influence user engagement.
- H2: Perceived reward value positively influences user engagement.
- H3: Perceived ease of use positively influences user engagement.
- H4: Perceived usefulness positively influences user engagement.
- H5: User engagement positively influences continuance intention.
- H6: Continuance intention positively influences sustainable QRIS use.

Treat these as hypotheses to be justified by literature and tested; do not present them as established facts.

## 7. Measurement and outcomes
Use validated scales from peer-reviewed literature, adapted carefully and translated/back-translated if needed. Pilot-test wording with UMKM users.

Primary outcomes:
- Usability (e.g., task completion, errors, perceived ease).
- Engagement (mission participation, learning completion, active weeks).
- Continuance intention.
- Sustainable use indicators, ideally based on verified activity rather than intention alone.

Secondary outcomes:
- QRIS knowledge quiz score.
- Digital payment confidence.
- Reward redemption and mission completion.

## 8. Experimental enhancement
If feasible, use a controlled comparison:
- Intervention: DigiUMKM with gamification.
- Comparison: equivalent QRIS learning content without gamification.

Random assignment is preferable where practical. Define baseline, intervention duration, follow-up, and primary outcome before collecting data. If randomization is not possible, describe the design as quasi-experimental and address confounding.

## 9. Sampling
Target owners/managers of rural MSMEs in selected East Java regencies. Define rural classification using an official statistical/administrative basis and state inclusion criteria clearly. Avoid convenience sampling claims of representativeness.

## 10. Ethics and privacy
- Obtain institutional ethics approval where required.
- Provide participant information and informed consent.
- Collect only necessary data.
- Do not collect payment PIN, OTP, banking passwords, account credentials, or customer personal data.
- Clearly distinguish self-reported, admin-verified, provider-verified, and simulated activity.
- Pseudonymize research data and define retention/deletion.
- Participation and reward must not be coercive.

## 11. Analysis plan
- Descriptive statistics and missing-data assessment.
- Measurement model: reliability, convergent validity, discriminant validity.
- Structural model: path coefficients, confidence intervals, effect sizes, predictive assessment.
- Mediation analysis for engagement where theoretically justified.
- Group comparison or intervention effect analysis if design and sample permit.
- Report limitations and avoid causal claims from cross-sectional data.

## 12. Research contribution
The paper should contribute a tested design artifact and empirical evidence about whether gamification-enabled support can improve engagement and sustained QRIS use among rural MSMEs. A prototype alone, without rigorous evaluation, is unlikely to be sufficient for a strong journal article.
